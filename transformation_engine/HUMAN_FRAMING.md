# Human Framing Layer

## Purpose

The Transformation Engine must not turn a promising opportunity directly into a
technical lesson. It first converts the opportunity into a human reason to care.

The framing sequence is:

1. Hook Experience
2. Viewer Question
3. Psychological Pull
4. Explanation Payoff
5. Visual Opening Plan

The technical mechanism is often the answer, not the doorway.

## Drama model

Drama is an editorial intensity control, not a scientific or neurological
measurement.

- hard floor: 4/10
- normal center: 5/10
- normal working range: 4-8
- 9-10 only when the real subject supports extreme stakes or spectacle
- 1-3 are not used for normal concept/story framing

Every concept records:

- drama capacity — how much truthful drama exists in the opportunity;
- drama target — how much the concept intends to use;
- drama source — the real property creating the intensity;
- drama constraint — what must not be exaggerated;
- hook level;
- story drama curve;
- tempo curve.

The target may not exceed capacity. A concept may not sit more than three points
below its assessed capacity, preventing high-drama opportunities from being
flattened into textbook framing.

## Drama pulse and tempo

Drama must change over time. A constant level is rejected.

Examples:

- 5 → 6 → 7 → 5
- 7 → 5 → 8 → 6
- 6 → 4 → 7 → 5 → 8

Tempo is independent from drama. Slow motion or a deliberate pause can be highly
dramatic. Tempo therefore receives its own 1-10 curve, which must also change
over the story.

A lower-drama beat is breathing room for understanding, not permission to
become boring.

## Truth boundary

The system looks for the strongest truthful manifestation of the opportunity.
It may intensify presentation, consequence, contrast, stakes and visual
selection, but it must not manufacture catastrophe or factual certainty.

Research must verify factual assumptions embedded in the Hook Experience,
stakes, drama source and Explanation Payoff before scripting treats them as
facts.

## Pipeline contract

The Human Framing contract is created during concept generation and preserved
through:

Transformation → Concept Triage → Human Concept Gate → Packaging → Research →
Story Planning → Script → Format → Voice Performance.

Concept Triage scores human pull, visual hook potential, payoff strength and
drama use in addition to the existing quality dimensions.

The Human Concept Gate displays the framing and exposes Rework criteria for
human-framing quality and truthful drama.

Story Planning assigns every beat:

- drama_level: 4-10
- tempo_level: 1-10

The Story Plan must pulse rather than remain flat and must reach the accepted
concept drama target.

Format Planning preserves the same principle in production treatment, and Voice
Performance receives the beat-level drama/tempo values as delivery context.

## Example: racing tyres in rain

Opportunity: tyres and water displacement.

Weak technical doorway:

> How Tread Geometry Shapes Wet-Road Grip

Human framing:

- Hook Experience: a car grips normally, reaches standing water, then suddenly
  loses control.
- Viewer Question: why did the grip disappear so suddenly?
- Psychological Pull: expectation violation + danger + cause/effect mystery.
- Explanation Payoff: understand slicks, loss of tyre-road contact,
  intermediates/full wets and why teams switch.
- Visual Opening Plan: control → wet patch → loss of grip → freeze/slowdown →
  explanation.
- Drama Capacity: high because sudden loss of control is visually consequential.
- Drama Constraint: do not imply every wet patch causes aquaplaning or a crash.

The system should preserve that human doorway even when the eventual factual
explanation becomes technical.
